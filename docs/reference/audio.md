# Audio reference

The full detail behind `audio/CLAUDE.md`, which keeps the contracts, and the
`yomi-sound` skill (`.claude/skills/yomi-sound/`), which keeps the workflow. Read this when writing a recipe, chasing a failed check,
or working on the narration tool. The design is `docs/designs/audio-pass.md`
(the recipe format is normative in its §5.6); the architecture brief
`docs/architecture/audio-pass.md` gives the reasons behind the tool and the
model choice, and `docs/features/audio-pass.md` the decision log.

## Layout in full

- `audio.toml`: sample rate, banks, size budgets, each loudness group's
  measure, target, tolerance and true-peak ceiling, encoding, the narration's
  post-processing.
- `sources.toml`: the provenance of every source (licence, author, url,
  original name and hash, excerpt, committed file's hash). Written by the
  tool, never by hand.
- `sources/<collection>/<id>.flac`: trimmed excerpts, mono, 48 kHz, 24-bit.
- `impulses/<name>.toml` and `.flac`: reverb impulses (`small_room`,
  `stone_hall`, `open_air`), registered as `impulse_<name>`, licence `own`.
- `sfx/<bank>/<name>.toml`: one recipe per cue `<bank>.<name>`
  (`world.rune.embed` is `sfx/world/rune.embed.toml`).
- `sfx.manifest.json`: generated by `sfx build` (every cue's bank, markers,
  durations and inputs hash; every bank's length and its encoded files' size
  and SHA-256).
- `.audition/`: gitignored, what `sfx audition` writes.

The banks are generated into `public/assets/audio/sfx/` (`<bank>.opus`,
`<bank>.mp3`, `<bank>.json`), and voice files are looked for in
`public/assets/voice/` for the voice budget: both are found from `--root`
(`<root>/../public/assets/...`).

A mistake prints one line naming the cue, source or field at fault and exits 1
(a build or check with several problems prints one line each); any other
failure (a corrupt file, say) prints its type and message on one line, never
a traceback.

## Recipes

The tool's reading of the format is in `tools/audio/yomi_audio/recipe.py`'s
docstring. In short: `variants`, `seed`, `group` (one of `audio.toml`'s),
optional `max_ms`; each `[[layer]]` is a `source`, a `pick` list or a `synth`,
then `start`/`length`, `reverse`, `pitch` (semitones, by resampling),
`filter`, `saturate`, `envelope`, `fade_in`/`fade_out`, `gain` and `offset`,
applied in that order; `[post] chain` runs on the mix (`compress`,
`saturate`, `reverb`, `delay`, `limit`, `trim`, `fade_out`), then the mix is
normalised to its group's target, always last: the gain is the lesser of the
one that reaches the loudness target and the one that leaves the true peak
just (0.05 dB) under the group's ceiling, so the ceiling always holds. A sound
much shorter than the 400 ms momentary window (a click) can need more gain to
reach a momentary target than its peak allows; it then lands under its
target, which the build accepts and reports as *peak-limited*. To bring such a
cue up, make it denser or longer (or compress it), not louder. Any number may
be `[lo, hi]`, drawn per variant; variant k depends only on the recipe and k,
and renders bit-identically every time. Changing a recipe's field order
changes nothing; changing its `seed` or what it draws changes every variant.

Tool conventions a recipe relies on: `trim`'s `threshold_db` is below the
mix's peak and it never cuts the start; `reverb` scales its impulse to unit
energy, so `wet_db` is the tail's level against the dry sound; a synth
sweep's `curve` defaults to `exp`; `q` defaults to 0.707.

## Building and checking

The code is `tools/audio/yomi_audio/build.py` (build, check) and `checks.py`
(the per-variant checks).

- **Incremental.** Each cue's `inputs` hash covers its recipe's text, the
  SHA-256 of every source and impulse file it names with each one's recorded
  licence and `sha256`, `audio.toml`, the tool's version and
  `tools/audio/uv.lock`. Every source of every recipe is verified on every
  build before anything else, so a licence or hash gone bad fails even a
  build that would render nothing. A cue whose hash differs from the
  manifest's (or which is new) is changed; a bank is touched when one of its
  cues changed or was removed, when `--cue`/`--bank` names it, or when one of
  its files is missing or differs from the manifest's record. Every cue of a
  touched bank is rendered again (rendering is deterministic) and the bank
  reassembled; untouched banks are left byte for byte. A build with nothing
  changed renders nothing and writes nothing. So a change to `audio.toml` or
  `uv.lock` (a new dependency) rebuilds everything.
- **Checks**, each failing the build, all reported in one run (a line each,
  naming `cue#k`, the value and the limit), with nothing written:
  - loudness within the group's tolerance of its target, or under it when
    peak-limited (above);
  - true peak at or under the group's ceiling;
  - length within the recipe's `max_ms`;
  - DC offset (the mean sample) at most 0.5% of full scale; a short low
    tone (half a cycle of 150 Hz in a 40 ms click) fails it, so high-pass it;
  - no two variants of a cue with a peak normalised cross-correlation above
    0.95 ("variants #i and #j correlate at …"): vary more than one filter's
    centre (pitch, a noise layer, a picked source). A one-variant cue is
    exempt;
  - every source the recipe names (drawn into a variant or not) registered,
    allowed and matching its SHA-256.
- **Layout.** In a bank, cues are in sorted id order and each cue's variants
  in order, marker `<cue>#<k>`; each variant is padded to a whole
  millisecond, with `gap_ms` of silence before every marker and after the
  last. The `.json` is Phaser's audio-sprite shape, start and end in seconds.
  Encoding: ffmpeg, mono 48 kHz, Opus VBR at `[encode.sfx] opus_kbps` and
  MP3 VBR at `mp3_q`, metadata stripped.
- **Budgets** (`audio.toml [budgets]`): `sfx_bytes_per_format` (all banks'
  `.opus` together, and all `.mp3`), `sources_bytes` (`audio/sources` and
  `audio/voices` together) and `voice_bytes_per_format`
  (`public/assets/voice/*.opus`, and `*.mp3`). The build checks the first two
  before writing anything; `sfx check` checks all three.
- **`sfx check`** writes nothing. It recomputes every cue's inputs hash,
  compares the recipe set with the manifest's cues, checks every bank's
  three files exist, that the `.opus` and `.mp3` match the manifest's size
  and SHA-256 and that the `.json` is exactly the sprite the manifest's
  markers and durations lay out (resources, starts and ends with `gap_ms`,
  `loop`), re-verifies every source, and checks the budgets. It never
  re-encodes: encoded bytes vary with the ffmpeg build, so the manifest's own
  record of them is the reference.

Every cue is real content (melee, shots, sweeteners, spells, falls,
footsteps, the world bank, the interface and the stings, steps 08 to 13 of
the audio-pass plan); the old placeholder tones were removed in step 18 and
no recipe or source names one.

## Sound design without ears

What the melee batch (step 08), the interface and stings (step 09),
the shots and sweeteners (step 10), the spells (step 11), the falls and
steps (step 12) and the world bank (step 13) found, for later sound work:

- **Layer shape.** A hit is a picked body (`pick` over several excerpts), a
  transient layer where the brief asks for an edge or tick, a short
  synthesized sine drop for weight, a short dense noise body, then
  `compress` → `reverb small_room` (wet −17 to −20) → `limit` → `trim` →
  `fade_out`. A swing is a picked real whoosh, high-passed, plus a swept
  band of noise for spread. Sweeteners are high-passed (metal) or short (the
  crit), so they sit round a hit, not on it.
- **Reaching an impact's level.** A short hit lands under −19 when its
  crest (true peak against the 400 ms window) is above about 18 dB, and the
  build then calls it peak-limited. A `limit` in `[post]` with a ceiling well
  under the mix's peak (−11 to −17 dB) flattens the transient enough; adding
  a quiet sustained layer helps less than expected.
- **DC.** A short sine drop or a low source cut mid-cycle fails the 0.5% DC
  check: high-pass the sine layer (30 to 80 Hz) and the low sources.
- **Judge spectra A-weighted.** Raw power shares say every hit is "all
  sub"; weighted by the ear (A-weighting), they show whether the edge, the
  crack or the ring is really there. The momentary loudness is K-weighted,
  so a big sub layer is nearly free in loudness and swamps a mix on large
  speakers: keep sub layers several dB under the body.
- **Look for clicks.** Some recordings carry a spike far above their sound
  (an excerpt's crest over 25 dB after high-passing): it pins the true peak
  and the variant stays quiet. Drop such excerpts rather than limit them.
- **medicinestorm's weapon textures** (192 kHz, long takes of several
  swings) are the best whooshes found, but each swing carries a large
  sub-bass wind thump on the microphone: high-pass at 200 to 700 Hz.
- **Clicks** (step 09). An impulse through a resonant band has almost no
  energy against its peak, so the click is all spike and lands far under
  its target: build a click's body from short decaying sines (the wood's
  modes) instead. A UI click of 45 ms stays peak-limited (about −31 LUFS-M)
  whatever is done, which suits a sound heard this often; keep its
  variants within a dB or two of each other with a `limit` well under the
  peak. To judge two short cues against each other (back softer than
  click), compare their loudest 50 ms A-weighted, not LUFS-M: the 400 ms
  window favours the longer one.
- **Stings** (step 09). Kenney's jingles are 0.3 to 1.8 s melodies in
  whole-tone and chromatic runs; a sting uses one as its phrase and
  extends it with synthesized chords (saws with a closing low-pass, some
  doubled by a detuned twin) and timpani (a falling sine plus a pink-noise
  slap). Find a jingle's notes with a harmonic-product-spectrum tracker and
  write the chord in its key; keep each sting's key in its recipe's first
  line (victory and level-up in G, defeat in D minor).
- A synth note whose envelope ends above about −50 dB clicks where its
  `length` cuts it: end the envelope lower or add a `fade_out`.
- `source add` refuses an `--end` past the end of a short file; leave
  `--start`/`--end` off to keep the whole of it.
- **`limit` is absolute** (step 10): its `ceiling_db` is dBFS of the mix
  *before* normalisation, so a ceiling above the mix's own peak does
  nothing. A layered mix of near-full-scale excerpts peaks around −6 to
  −10 dBFS after `compress`; the shots needed −17 to −21 and the magic hit
  −24 to reach their targets. Lower it until the build stops calling the
  cue peak-limited.
- **Element sweeteners** (step 10) play 30 ms after a weapon hit, so they
  keep out of its low end: high-pass every layer at 450 to 900 Hz, and
  cascade two high-passes (each is 12 dB/octave) where a source has a
  strong 150 to 300 Hz body (the flame). Under 300 Hz they hold 0 to 1% of
  their energy (poison, meant low, 5%). A chime's synthesized partials
  vanish under any noisy layer at a similar gain: keep a dark swell 10 dB
  or more under the chime, low-passed.
- **Excerpts with lead silence.** Check an excerpt's onset before adding it
  (some originals open with 50 to 80 ms of silence, which delays the cue
  against the hit); re-add it with `--start` at the onset.
- **A miss is not a hit.** medicinestorm's passbys carry the same sub wind
  thump as its swings, and a soft impact pitched down is a body thump:
  cascade high-passes (400 to 500 Hz on the passby, 260 Hz on the thud)
  and keep the thud 20 dB under the whizz.
- **Spells** (step 11). Normalisation always lands a cue on its group's
  target, so no recipe can make one cue louder in LUFS-M max than its
  group (the fireball's "loudest" brief): make it louder for longer
  instead. A sustained roar bed under the fireball's tail put its loudness
  over 3 s 1.3 dB or more above every other impact; `gainDb` in `CUES`
  (`src/scenes/sounds.ts`) is the runtime lever if more is wanted.
- **Setting `limit`.** After a first audition, raise (or lower) the
  ceiling by the variant's true peak plus about 2.5 dB: that lands the
  peak near −3 to −6 dBTP, keeping the transient, where a ceiling far down
  leaves a peak of −9 to −12 and a squashed attack.
- **Synth tones read as synth.** A sustained sine or low-index FM partial
  is a pure line on the spectrogram and sounds electronic. For bells use
  FM with a high index (2 to 3) falling to near 0 and an envelope down
  9 to 12 dB within 120 to 160 ms; real voices (a hummed chord pitched to
  a major triad) make a choir pad that a saw pad cannot. Keep friendly
  bells' 2–5 kHz share down with a low-pass at 2.6 to 3 kHz and a −6 dB
  peak at 3.5 kHz (FM at ratio 3 puts a partial at 4× the carrier).
- **Casts lighter than impacts.** A hum or pad under 300 Hz makes a cast
  as heavy as its impact; put the hum an octave up (390 to 470 Hz) and
  cascade high-passes at 240 Hz on a hummed root. Measured: casts hold
  0–34% of their raw power under 300 Hz, their impacts 47–94%.
- **Acid and poison** are told apart by band, not texture: acid has no
  energy under 300 Hz (a splash, a high-passed sizzle at 2.5 kHz twice,
  frying crackle), poison two thirds of it there (a pitched-down soft
  puff, an air hiss low-passed and closing from 2 kHz to 700 Hz, a deep
  drone, bubbles pitched down an octave).
- **Spell-impact packs.** lentikula's spell impacts (in untamedpixels'
  150 Combat Sound Effects) open with a charge of 0.1 to 0.9 s before the
  burst: cut impacts at the burst and use the lead-ins as cast material
  (the storm cast is Lightning Spell Impact 2's charge).
- `sfx audition` with a single cue writes its files straight into `--out`;
  with several, a folder per cue.
- **Footsteps** (step 12) play every 140 ms (`MOVE_STEP_MS`), so a walk
  is one cue after another: keep each step's energy in its first 140 ms
  (measured 88 to 100%). Kenney's RPG Audio footsteps are a heel and a toe
  70 to 110 ms apart; damp the toe 8 to 14 dB with an envelope, or the
  walk ticks twice as fast. A soft heavy impact under a troll's step rings
  as a 90 Hz sine: cut its envelope to −40 dB by 240 ms and keep it 3 to
  5 dB under the foot's slap, so a run of steps does not boom. Steps are
  short, so they come in peak-limited until a `limit` sits 14 to 21 dB
  down.
- **Falls are told apart by what speaks**: the human by no voice (a breath
  13 dB down, then gear), goblinoids by a pitched-up yelp with a squeal
  over it (2–5 kHz, A-weighted 26–86%), the brute by a groan whose
  low-pass closes as it dies and a thud with a sub sweep (59–92% of raw
  power under 300 Hz), the spider by clicks and snaps with no voiced
  harmonics, the salamander by a hiss and a splash. A pitched-down layer
  plays slower, so it uses less of its source: trim an excerpt to
  length × 2^(pitch/12) and keep the budget.
- **One-take cues** (`death.horror`): the reversed swell must end where the
  fall lands; `reverse` applies after `start`/`length`, so cut the source
  first, then reverse.
- **World cues** (step 13) play between turns over the world theme, so the
  stingers share their material to sound like one family (the war drum
  and taiko of Freesound 235453 and 347126 in `confront`, `sighted` and
  `lost`; the tolling bell 454855 in `threatened` and `lost`) and the
  encampment's handiwork stays dry (`small_room`). A brass stab can be a
  real horn pitched to two notes a semitone apart, with a quiet saw
  cluster under it, rather than synthesized brass. Keep the warning toll
  apart from the hero's (another bell, about 6 semitones higher, no
  drone, open air rather than a stone hall).
- **An anvil is all ring** (434339: 89% of its power above 3 kHz): pitch
  it down 2.5 to 4.5 semitones, cut its ring with a peak (3.4 kHz, −8 dB)
  and put a punch, cascade-high-passed at 160 Hz, under it for the
  hammer's weight; the craft still holds 41–61% of its A-weighted energy
  at 2–5 kHz.
- **Pitch shortens a layer.** `length` is cut before `pitch` resamples, so
  a layer pitched up p semitones lasts length × 2^(−p/12): a reversed
  swell meant to end on a pop must be placed by its pitched length (the
  rune extract's bell, 570 ms at +3 to +4, lasts 452–479 ms, against the
  pop at 445–460).
- **FM notes need a fade.** An FM bell whose envelope ends at −45 dB
  leaves a step where `length` cuts it, visible as a vertical line in the
  spectrogram after the reverb: add `fade_out = 60` (or end the envelope
  at −55).
- **Sighted against its horn.** A sustained horn sets the momentary
  loudness, so drum hits low-passed at 700 Hz under it sat 16–22 dB down
  A-weighted; low-passed at 1.4 kHz and 9 to 12 dB over the horn they sit
  1–4 dB under it.

## Narration engines

- `tools/audio/yomi_audio/tts/`: one adapter (`base.py`: `RenderJob`,
  `Engine`, `EMOTION_MAP`, `exaggeration`, `qwen_instruction`,
  `ADAPTER_VERSION`) and an engine module each: `chatterbox` (clones the
  emotion's clip, `exaggeration` by intensity, `cfg_weight`),
  `chatterbox_turbo` (clones one clip, renders `[sigh]` and the other tags,
  ignores exaggeration), `qwen3_custom` (a preset speaker with an
  instruction) and `qwen3_design` (a voice from a description; used only to
  design clips). All four run on **mlx-audio 0.6.5** (MLX, Apple Silicon);
  none needs PyTorch. An engine imports mlx only when it first renders.
- Pins (`tts/models.py`; the chosen ones are copied into `voices.toml`, and a
  pytest holds them equal). Each MLX conversion is pinned to a commit, and so
  is the upstream model whose card governs the licence. Checked on the cards

  cloned from, with each narrator's `design.json` of similarities). It reuses
  a narrator's `designed/` only while its `design.json` covers every emotion
  at the same candidate count, and with `--redesign` never. The bake-off's
  narrators, reference text, lines and configurations are constants in
  `yomi_audio/voice.py`.
- Every clip goes through `audio.toml`'s `[voice]` chain (`voice.postprocess`).

## Designing a voice

`uv run --project tools/audio --extra tts yomi-audio voice design <voice>
[--emotions E,...] [--candidates N] [--neutral WAV] [--out DIR]`
(`yomi_audio/design.py`) makes emotion reference-clip candidates for a voice
of `voices.toml`, for the author to pick **by ear**:

- The reference is the voice's neutral clip: `--neutral`, else the file its
  `[<voice>.clips].neutral` names (`voices/<voice>/neutral.flac`); without one
  it refuses, naming the file.
- For each emotion (default every one the voice declares but neutral),
  Qwen3-TTS VoiceDesign reads `[<voice>.design].reference_text` from its
  `description` with the emotion's instruction (`EMOTION_MAP`) appended,
  seeds `seed` to `seed + N - 1` (N default 4), each through the `[voice]`
  chain.
- Each candidate's speaker similarity to the reference (cosine of
  Chatterbox's voice-encoder embeddings) is printed; one below **0.75** is
  another voice and is discarded, never written. The rest go to
  `<out>/<emotion>_<k>.wav` (default `<out>` `.audition/voice-<voice>/`, which
  is gitignored) beside `neutral.wav`, `design.json` and `review.md` (a
  table per emotion: candidate, seed, similarity, loudness, length; and how
  to answer). An emotion with no candidate left says to try
  `--candidates 8`. A later run for some emotions replaces only theirs; a new
  reference, `design` table or design-engine fingerprint drops them all.
  An empty `--emotions` is refused.
- Renders are reproducible for a seed and the pinned revision to within
  float noise (step 15 measured at most 0.005 sample difference against
  step 03's renders of the same seeds).
- The picks are committed by hand as `voices/<voice>/<emotion>.flac`, each
  with a `sources.toml` entry (licence `own`, `design = {...}` provenance),
  and listed in `[<voice>.clips]`. Chatterbox (`chatterbox`) clones the
  line's emotion clip; **Chatterbox Turbo**, the narrator's engine, clones
  only the neutral clip, so the narrator lists `neutral` alone (step 15
  measured Turbo cloning an emotion clip: the voice drifted, similarity to
  `A_0` 0.81 to 0.84 against 0.92 to 0.96, and only the pace followed).

### A new fixed character

A new fixed character is a new table in `voices.toml` (engine, pinned model, emotions, `design`, `clips`, `fallback`), its clips (designed with `voice design` and picked by ear, then committed with a `sources.toml` entry, licence `own`), and beats naming it; `VoiceId` in `src/story/narration.ts` grows with it. Model pins live in `tools/audio/yomi_audio/tts/models.py` and `voices.toml`, held equal by a pytest; a new model's licence must allow its output in a commercial game.

The full rules `narrationProblems` enforces: no duplicate ids, a voice for every line, no leader (speaker or portrait) and no `{…}` template in what is spoken, only known tags and only for a tag engine (`chatterbox_turbo`), an emotion the voice lists, intensity (0 to 1) and take in range, nothing empty. Tutorial tips are not voiced. Line ids match `[a-z0-9_.]+`.

## Rendering in detail

`scripts/narration.ts` (`npm run narration:export`) refuses a broken beat
and writes the gitignored `audio/narration.jobs.json`, a job per voiced line
with its seed and input hash (`lineHash`). `yomi-audio voice render`
(`render.py`) renders only the jobs whose hash differs from
`audio/voice.manifest.json`, has no entry there or whose files are missing:
the engine the voice table names clones the voice's clip for the emotion
(else its neutral clip), the `[voice]` chain post-processes it (high-pass,
trim, -18 LUFS-I, true-peak ceiling), ffmpeg encodes
`public/assets/voice/<line>.opus` (mono 32 kbps, `voip`) and `.mp3` (VBR
`-q:a 7`), and the manifest records the hash, duration and
`engine@revision`. Files and entries of lines no longer in the jobs are
deleted; `README.md` stays. It refuses a job file whose `adapter_version`
differs from `voices.toml`'s or the adapter's (run the export again).

`audio/voices.toml` is read by `readVoices` (TypeScript) and
`design.read_voice` (the tool): narrator A on Chatterbox Turbo (gate G1),
`[narrator.clips]` naming only the neutral clip (`A_0`).
