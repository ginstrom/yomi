# The scene layer

Loaded when working in `src/scenes/`. Scenes are views: a scene turns input into calls on the domain modules and plays back what they return, and decides no outcome.

## Scenes and how they hand over

Scenes are `Boot` → `Menu` → `Party` ⇄ `Creation` → `World` ⇄ `Battle`, with `Encampment` ⇄ `GuildHall` (⇄ `Creation`), `TradingPost` and `Crafting` over `World`, `Exchange` over `World` (opened from the footer and the character panel), and `Crafting` over `World` alone from its Craft button, and `Story` over `World`, `Battle` or `Encampment`. `Story` is the story told at a moment (`tellStory` in `StoryScene.ts`, or `showStory` with what `storyFor` already chose, as `Battle` does to save what it shows with the turn): it pauses the scene under it and plays its items, a cutscene a beat at a time (a click, Space or Enter goes on, Esc skips the rest) and a tip as a panel with a ring round its anchor, whose Turn tutorial off drops the tips still waiting; then it resumes the scene, which saves. A voiced beat (`beatLine`: a `line` the voice manifest has) speaks on the voice bus as it is shown, `LINE_DELAY_MS` after it is drawn, with the music ducked under it; Next cuts the line with a short fade and Esc stops it and skips, leaving the story or shutting the scene down stops it and releases the duck, and a beat whose file is missing or failed to load, or the Voice slider at 0, is silent, the beat's text staying as the subtitle; the lines load in `StoryScene.preload` with the backdrops, never in `BootScene`. `World` raises its moments as it opens and after everything that passes time, `Battle` as each of the player's turns begins, `Encampment` as it opens. New on the menu starts `Party`, which asks whether to take the ready-made party (shown under its button as portraits and names, a hover telling what a member comes to) or to create one's own and then shows the roster: a row for each place up to the party limit, where a member is edited or removed and an empty place leads to `Creation` or to the prerolls, a row of faces where a click adds that character (one already in the party is greyed out, as names must be unique). `Party` holds the party as drafts and passes them to `Creation` and back as scene data (`{ drafts, index? }`, `index` being the member to edit); The roster also holds the encampment's name, typed there (every key goes to it) and carried through `Creation` and back as scene data (`encampmentName`); the roster's Tutorial box (ticked by default, carried the same way as `tutorial`) sets the new game's tutorial, which the menu's Options turn on and off over a game (`Menu` is handed the `game` for it); Begin makes the characters and a `GameState` of them, in an encampment of that name, in the first empty save slot, asking which to overwrite when all three are full, and starts `World`. `Creation` walks four steps (attributes, skills, perks, name and portrait) over one draft, the perks step showing the tree view across the whole panel (the summary gives way to it), headed "Perk points: N of 3", where only a tier-1 node answers a click, taking it or giving it back, every control calling a function of `creation.ts`; a plus or minus held down repeats as a held key does, the hold being the scene's, since each change redraws the button. The menu's Continue resumes the game paused under it, or with none loads the slot written last; Load lists the three slots and starts `World`, or `Battle` with the saved battle (`saved` in its scene data), for the one picked; Manual lays the player's manual over the page as HTML (`manualOverlay.ts`, loaded only then, rendered from `docs/manual/README.md` by `manualHtml.ts` with `marked`), taking every key until its Back to game link or Esc closes it, so the game under the menu is left as it was. `Encampment` is an overlay over the paused map, opened by the footer's Enter button (named for the encampment) while the party stands there: a picture of the place, each facility of `facilities` (encampment.ts) drawn on its plot at its level, or as an empty plot while not built (`encampmentView.ts`); a click on one shows what it does and costs, with Build or Raise, and for the guild hall, the trading post and the workshop, once built, a button to go in, which starts `GuildHall`, `TradingPost` or `Crafting`, each starting `Encampment` again when it closes; Leave starts `World` again, as the party under its column may have changed. Every scene that acts for a party takes `party?: string` (its id) in its scene data, resolves it with `selectedParty(game, data.party)` and passes it on to the scene it starts; `Inventory` is handed the members themselves. `GuildHall` is a column of faces for each party at home (`homeParties`) and one for the reserves (`hallColumns.ts` maps a pick and a click onto `moveHero`, `swap` and `exchange`): a click picks a hero, a click on a hero of another column swaps the two, a click on that column's heading moves it there; Form party, Rename (every key goes to the name, the first replacing the old one as a selected field's text is and a Ctrl, Cmd or Alt chord typing nothing, `typeInRename`, Enter keeps it, Esc cancels) and Disband act on the picked hero's party (`formParty`, `renameParty`, `disband`); and Recruit, which offers the pre-rolls not in the company or Create new, which starts `Creation` with `returnTo: 'GuildHall'` and the game (the company's names are taken), and `Creation` starts `GuildHall` again with its draft to recruit, or none on Cancel. The scene resolves its party with `homeParty(game, data.party)` and the encampment, trading post and crafting screens say "For: <name>" (`actingFor`, encampmentView.ts) while more than one party is at home. `Exchange` is two parties on one hex as two rows of faces and two baggage lists (`exchangeView.ts`: partners on a hex, a pick and a click as an `ExchangeMove`), every change through `exchange`; it is opened by the footer's Exchange button or by the Inventory tab's, passed to the panel in its data as `exchange`, and resumes `World` with `{ exchanged: true }`, which rebuilds the party column. Close starts `Encampment` again. `TradingPost` has Buy and Sell tabs of paged lines (the Buy tab filtered by slot and family, or by its Runes button after the slots, which lists the runes the post's level sells, opening on weapons and back to it whenever the tab is shown, with eight lines a page under the filter; the baggage first when selling, then the packs, then the stock's materials, then the pouch's runes, sold one at a time, Sell unfiltered); a click on a line shows its stats (a rune's effect in each place) and price and a button that confirms. `Crafting` has two tabs, Modify, which it opens on, and Runes. Modify lists the party's items a modifier fits, worn, carried and in the baggage; a click on one lists the modifiers and grades on offer where the party stands, a click on one of those shows what it changes (`gearCompare.ts`, attacks a turn included), and Work it confirms. Runes lists the items with a socket over the rune pouch (with none, it says which gear has sockets, `noSocketsHint`, read off `socketsOf` over the catalog by `socketSources`; hovering a pouch row shows what the rune does in each place): a click on an item shows its sockets, each rune with what it does there and Extract; each rune of the pouch has Embed (into the item clicked) and Fuse; a click on an action, greyed but still clickable when refused, previews it with its cost or refusal (`runecraftView.ts`), and Confirm does it. Opened from the map's Craft button (`resume: 'World'`), a field craft or a field embed stops the scene and resumes `World` with what it made and the day's events (`CraftedData`), which the map reports as it does camping. The footer has two rows, the time buttons over the actions: Next day (`passDay`: every party carries on its order, a walk cut short or a camp resuming) and Until something happens (`advance`, stopping at an interrupt, the party it concerns selected and centred, or after `UNTIL_LIMIT` (30) days passed quietly, which the message says) show while the game is active and the selected party stands on no monster location (confronting it or, after a scout or a wait, not), each refusing with `advanceRefusal`'s reason, and then selecting and centring the party whose monsters block the days (`blockingParty`), whose footer offers Scout, Wait and Attack (a map click, Return home, and every footer action that passes a day, Scout and Wait included where another party's monsters block it (`actionBlocked`), Camp, Camp until healed, a field Craft and the buildings, refuse the same way, giving no order); they save as the days are passed and then play the selected party's steps as a journey plays, the party list's order line saying the order walked by until the walk ends (`deployedParties`' `shown`), every other party jumping to its hex when the walk ends (only the selected party's walk is animated); the words are `describeDays` (`worldEvents.ts`), which lead with the party's name ("Second Company: arrived at Kobold Scouts.") once the company has two parties or more; a day the selected party spends on its own action (Scout, Wait, Camp, Camp until healed, a building) is told in its own words followed by `describeOthers`, what else the day brought (another party's event or a notable one, whose party is selected and centred as for an interrupt). The map opens on the party its scene data names, else (a load) on the first whose monsters block the days, else the first (`openingParty`), its opening line naming that party where there are several (`describeStanding`). Beside them in that row, Return home (away from the encampment; not "Home", the key that centres the view) walks the selected party home by a `home` order and Garrison (on a held resource it does not garrison yet) sets a `garrison` order, passing no day, and Exchange (another party on the selected party's hex) opens `Exchange`. The row below offers, among monsters, Scout, Wait and Attack, and anywhere else Camp, Camp until healed and Craft, with Build Watchtower where the land would take one, Build Outpost on a held site without one (a click without the stock says what it costs) and Enter at the encampment. A click on a hex where another party stands sends the selected party to meet it (`meetTarget`, the first there in list order; the header's hint says "Meet <name>" over it), any other hex is travel; a walk (a click, Return home, a meet: `walkTo` and `followOrder` in worldMap.ts) is told by `describeWalk` and, like the days, selects and centres the party its interrupt concerns. The world map draws the land as the player knows it (`seen`): black where never explored, and elsewhere the blight as a tint a level over the grass, a growth on corrupted land (`map_blight`, one animated sprite a hex) and a faint line along clean land's edge, under a veil where out of sight, rendered into a texture whenever that changes; a location shows once known (a monster location sighted, a resource explored), faded out of sight, and the header's blighted share is of the land explored; a held site's name is starred and green, with "outpost" under it where one stands, and a threatened one's orange with the days until it falls; hovering a hex shows the route a click would walk and, while nothing is hovered and no walk plays, the map draws the selected party's standing route instead, in amber, what is left of its travel order to its destination (`standingRoute` in `partyList.ts`; gone once the order ends, and back after a reload, as the order is saved), and at the view's top right, an info panel on the hex (`hexInfo.ts`). Down the map's right side, in an opaque strip the view ends at (`PARTY_LIST_W`), is the list of the company's parties ("Your party" while there is one, else each by its name): an entry a party, its leader's portrait with its banner at its edge and, with two parties or more, its name shortened across its foot (`entryName`), member count, hit point bar, its order ("Travelling to Troll Lair", "Camping", "Idle", "Before the …", two small lines under the bar, in full on hover) and a red "!" while it stands before monsters, the selected one framed in gold; a click on one selects it, so that the left column and Home follow it, and centres the view on it, and hovering one shows its summary in the info panel. When the game is won or lost a screen covers the map: End (to `Menu`, the save kept) and Continue (endless) after a victory, Main Menu after the encampment falls. Attacking a monster location starts `Battle` with that location's id; a victory clears the location and returns to `World`, and a defeat wipes the party out (`wipeParty`) and, with heroes left elsewhere, saves and returns to `World` with the first party selected, or with none left deletes the game's slot and goes back to `Menu`. `Inventory` is the character panel (its item tooltip lists an item's sockets and runes below its stats, and its stat lines, on the Character and Inventory tabs, carry a `Wards:` line while any ward is above 0, and the Character tab an `On a hit:` line for the main hand's runes (`onHitLines`), which the Inventory tab, short of room, leaves to the weapon's tooltip), an overlay showing one party member at a time in four tabs (Character, Skills, Abilities, Inventory; `PanelTab` adds `'trees'` for the tree view): over `Battle` (C, S, I; B within the panel), where gear can be changed for an action point but skill points, nodes and abilities are only shown, and over `World` (C), where all can be spent and gear is changed freely, through the baggage. The Character tab's line under the name is `heroTitle` (`trees.ts`: "Level 9 · Heavy Knight / Paladin"). The Skills tab lists the skills, with "N skill points to spend" over them in the portrait's "+" green while a raise can be made there (`skillPointsLine`, `skillsTab.ts`; a greyed "+" pointed at gives `skillRaiseRefusal`'s reason under the rules), and only the passives a character has taken, spells aside, stacked by the lines each description wraps to (`passiveRows`, cut to a line each and then "+N more" when they would not fit), and "Perk points: N" (gold on the world map while some open node is affordable); its Skill Trees button (or P) swaps the panel's content for the tree view, on the tree of the hero's calling, with no panel tab of its own but a tab for each of the four trees (or keys 1 to 4), where a click on an open node takes it and Esc or Back steps back to the Skills tab. The Abilities tab lists the actives known, the spells and then the techniques (`abilityRows`, `abilityList.ts`: kind, AP with discounts and for the weapon held, mana, recharge, and a damaging spell's dice with the caster's power, `shownDescription`), twelve a page in two columns of fixed-height rows (`abilityRowRect`, with a pager over them past twelve), the mana every hero has and the spell skill, gear included; K is unbound; on the world map a heal's Cast button casts it (`castOnWorldMap`), a heal of one asking for a portrait to be clicked (`worldCast`). It is handed the whole party (`PanelMember`s: each character, and in battle what its gear may do) and the member to open on, and when it closes it tells the scene under it which member it last showed, as the data of the resume.

## The party column

The party's portraits run down the left of `World`, `Battle` and `Inventory` alike (`PartyColumn`, placed by `partyPanel.ts`), so they seem to stay put under the panel. One member is the active one, marked in gold: in battle the selected unit, on the world map only the member a panel opens for. A click on a portrait makes its member active and a click on the active member's opens its panel (`portraitClick`); in battle a member that cannot be selected (spent, fallen, or during the enemy's turn) has its panel opened at once. A click on a portrait beside the open panel turns the panel to that member. `Menu` is an overlay over `World` or `Battle` (Esc). Both are told which scene to resume by their `resume` scene data; overlays pause the scene under them and resume it on close.

## The battle screen

`BattleScene` is a view over the engine: it turns clicks into `Action`s, calls `engine.step`, and plays back the returned events. It must not decide outcomes. Every battle is seeded, by the page's address or else at random, the enemy's RNG with the seed plus one, so that any battle can be saved and replayed from its log. A click on a foe always means a weapon attack, from where the unit stands if it can, else after the walk into reach that leaves the AP for it (`planAttack`), and nothing at all when no such walk exists, as a walk that cannot end in the attack is a plain move, a click on a hex (hovering that foe says why in the header hint, in the warning colour, `noStrikeHint`: "Too few AP to reach and strike", or "Too few AP to strike" for a foe in range; the hint wraps at the window's width and the usual one is kept to one line at 840 wide, as the party's column covers a second); a spell or an ability (a hero's technique) is used from the Skills button (M), shown for a unit with magic or abilities ("Skills (mana)" for one with magic), which opens the list of its skills whenever the player has control (`skillMenu.ts`: spells in `ALL_SPELL_IDS` order, then abilities in `ABILITY_IDS` order, each with its mana and the AP the unit pays now, `spellApCost` or `abilityApCost` for the weapon held, and "(ready in N)" while recharging), each greyed when the engine lists no use of it now, its hover saying why (the weapon it needs, "Once a battle", "Recharging (ready in N)", "Needs N mana", "Needs N AP", "No foe in range" or "No target in range", from the engine's state and the battle's units, and "Not now" only when none of these holds), and picked by a click or its number; the skill then asks for its target even when there is only one (`targeting` holds what the next board click picks a target for, an attack or a `Skill`): a foe for a strike or a foe status, the user for a self status (Sprint) or an area round it (Taunt, Consecrate), a hex for any other area, drawn with the engine's `abilityArea`; hovering an area's centre outlines the area and shows the odds on every unit it would touch, allies included (a status laid with no roll shows its letter), and a foe a strike is aimed at shows its odds (`previewCast`, `previewAbility`). The pointer over a valid target is the crossed swords for a weapon attack, a hostile spell or an ability, or a green cross for a friendly spell or a status the user lays on itself (`spellCursor`, `CURSORS.heal` in `cursors.ts`). Nameplates show each unit's statuses as coloured letters, bold and outlined, in a row centred over the unit's name (the probe's `marks`), in a layer of their own over every plate and never smaller than 12 px on screen however far the board is scaled down (`statusMarks.ts`). Hovering a standing foe that has abilities or wards shows a small panel beside it (`foeInfoLines`; the probe's `foeInfo`), its name the title and each ability's description dimmer under it, drawn at its own size however far the board is scaled down, cleared as each event plays and drawn again when playback ends. A hit's on-hit damage floats over the defender as "+3 fire" at its own size (`bonusText`, in a layer of its own), and a Life rune's healing refreshes the attacker's hit points; the nameplates' status letters are redrawn for a status a rune laid as for one an attack did (`changesStatusMarks`). A `restore` event floats its hit points and mana and refills the bar, an Unbreakable save floats "Endures", and a `revived` cast outcome stands the figure back up (`standUp`: full opacity, idle, plate back). A spell aimed at `'fallen'` (Revive) is picked by a click on the fallen ally's hex (`aimedAt`; a standing unit on the hex offers nothing), the fallen staying on the board faded, and `skillMenuRows` greys a once-a-battle spell already in `usedOnce` with "Once a battle". The six stage-3 spells have looks of their own in `SPELL_LOOKS`. The character panel's Abilities tab cuts each description to two lines (`ABILITY_DESCRIPTION_LINES`) and pages its rows so that a hero with many actives fits at 840×560. A win at a monster location rolls its runes into the pouch when the battle ends (`takeRunes`, seeded from the `battle_start` seed in the log, so a reloaded battle finds the same; a battle with no location rolls none) and names them with the items on the "Found:" line. A routine's strikes, a technique's included, play as successive attacks with the weapon's look, an elemental or holy technique's hit flashing in its spell's colour (`strikeFlash`), and an area ability or a status laid with no roll (`AbilityEvent`) plays through the spell-effect path: its hexes flash and each unit touched shows how it fared (Volley an arrow at each hex, Taunt a red ring on the user). Scenes use Phaser's `RESIZE` scale mode, so each one positions everything in a `layout()` method that reruns on resize; the game is held at a minimum of 840×560 (`MIN_GAME_SIZE` in `src/main.ts`), below which the page scrolls. `World` and `Battle` lay their map, board, log and buttons out to the right of the party's column (`PARTY_PANEL_W`).

## Changing gear in battle and on the map

Gear can be changed mid-battle. The inventory opens for the selected unit (I or C) and edits its `Character` directly (`equipFromBackpack`, `unequipToBackpack`), and when it closes `BattleScene` sends the engine an `equip` action carrying the newly derived stats, one for each member whose gear was changed while the panel was open (its portraits turn it from member to member), since the engine knows stats and not items; the action also lists the items changed (`gearChanges`), which the engine only copies into the `equip` event. New stats never add mana to spend: the engine keeps what is left, within the new maximum. Items can be dropped on that unit's hex and picked up from it the same way: the inventory's Ground section edits that hex's pile (`dropFromBackpack`, `dropEquipped`, `pickUp`; right-click or Ctrl+click drops, a click on a ground item picks it up into the backpack, and a pile deeper than the grid scrolls with the wheel)., and the same `equip` action lists what was `dropped` and `pickedUp`, again only for the log. An item can also be dragged between the backpack, the equipment slots and the ground, a drop making one of those same changes or nothing (`gearDrag.ts`; dropped on its slot it is equipped there, so a ring goes on whichever ring slot it is dropped on, the optional slot of `equipFromBackpack`); a primary click therefore acts on release, and a press that moves more than `DRAG_THRESHOLD_PX` is a drag. Equipping, stowing, dropping and picking up share one cost: a change costs `EQUIP_AP_COST` (1 AP), paid once: further changes are free until the unit moves or attacks or its turn ends (`equipCost`). `legalActions` never lists `equip`, so the AI policies do not change gear.

On the world map the same panel changes gear at no cost, with the party's baggage where the ground is (`WorldScene` passes `gearApCost: 0` and `ground: <the party>.baggage`): a click takes an item from the baggage into the open member's backpack, a right- or Ctrl+click puts one in, and so an item passes from member to member. Nothing is told to anyone when the panel closes, as the characters and the baggage were edited in place. Only a Life rune's `maxHp` changes maximum hit points (in armour or a shield; in a weapon Life heals on a hit) (`maxHp` adds the worn items'), so every change to what a character wears ends with `clampHp(character, maxBefore)`, the maximum read before the change: when it moves the hero keeps the same share of its hit points (the user's choice, both ways), `min(max, max(1, round(hp × max / maxBefore)))` (never above the new maximum, never to 0 from a positive value, so taking Life gear off and putting it back leaves `hp` where it was, within rounding, and a full hero stays full), and a hero at 0 stays at 0. That share rule is outside battle; in battle the rule is the engine's, whose `equip` only lowers hit points to the new maximum, so Life armour put on mid-fight heals nothing, and the character panel changes gear there by the cap rule (`HpRule` 'cap', which `BattleScene` hands the panel as each member's `hpRule`), so it shows what the engine holds. `equipFromBackpack`, `unequipToBackpack` and `dropEquipped` do it, by the rule they are given; a new way of changing what a character wears must too. In battle the engine's `equip` clamps `hp` to the new maximum. A ring (`Item.ring`) fits either ring slot: clicked, the first if it is empty, else the second, else in place of the first; dragged, the one it is dropped on.

A scene that redraws itself after a change (`Inventory`, `Party`, `Creation`) does so on the next tick, as the redraw destroys the button whose click handler asked for it, and its new buttons take clicks only from the frame after.

## Probes

Every scene but `Boot` has a `probe()`: plain data telling where its controls are on screen and what state it shows, typed in `src/testHook.ts`. In dev builds `main.ts` installs the hook that puts the probes within the in-browser tests' reach as `window.__yomi`; a production build leaves it out. Game code must not read the hook or the probes. A control that a test needs to click goes in its scene's probe.

## Assets the scenes load

each directory's `README.md` records provenance and licence and should be updated when files are added. `BootScene` preloads the four sound-effect banks as audio sprites (`BANKS`, `bankFiles` in `sounds.ts`) and the two themes (`MUSIC_KEYS`; `BattleScene` and `WorldScene` each start theirs through `soundPlayer.ts`, `music_battle` and `music_world_map`, paused while the menu is over them), and installs the output limiter, and the sprite atlas with its animations (`atlas.png`, `atlas.json`, `atlas.anims.json`), from which everything that moves is shown: units, what they hold and the world map's locations. A frame is named `<sprite>/<frame>` and an animation `<sprite>/<anim>` (`spriteFrame`, `spriteAnim`), and the animations themselves are written in the sprites' sources, not in the scenes. A unit is drawn as the atlas's sprite named for its `UnitKind`, with `idle` and `walk` animations, so a new unit kind needs a sprite of that name with both and an experience value in `FOE_XP`. Every hero is the `warrior` body, bare, with sprites laid under and over it for what it wears (`WORN_SPRITES`, and `WORN_BACK_SPRITES` for the cloak's cape behind it) and holds (`GEAR_SPRITES`), all in `unitLook.ts` by the item's icon, from `art/gear/`, and drawn in `heroLook`'s order (cape, body, armour, cloak, head, main hand, off hand, whichever way it faces); an item whose icon has none is worn or held unseen, so a new weapon or shield icon, or armour, head or cloak icon, needs an entry there and a sprite, or `unitLook.test.ts` fails; `BattleScene` puts a hero's gear on its figure again whenever the character panel closes. A strike's lunge, a hit's flash and the battle's other effects are tweens in `BattleScene`, as they were never frames: a blade or axe swung about the fist (`UnitFigure.swing`, the sprites in `SWUNG_GEAR`, turned about the body's `grip` point, which the atlas carries on each frame as Phaser's `customData` and `pointFromAnchor` reads), a slash, arrows and wand bolts in flight, each spell's missile and impact (as `battleEffects.ts` picks; an arrow, a missile and a burst are sprites of the atlas from `art/effects/`, turned along their flight about their `tip`, a missile and a burst tinted in the spell's colour, while flares, trails, clouds, motes and rings are drawn shapes), shots and spells aimed at and flying from each unit's `chest` point, a hit's knock or a miss's dodge, a step's bob, and an outline pulsed on whoever a blow or a spell lands on (`UnitFigure.pulseOutline`, Phaser's Glow filter, drawn only by WebGL). `InventoryScene`, `TradingPostScene` and `CraftingScene` load one icon per `ItemIcon` from `assets/sprites/icon_<icon>.png`, keyed by `ITEM_ICON_KEYS`, and also every rune's icon and the two socket pips (`RUNE_ICON_KEYS`, `SOCKET_PIP_KEYS`; `RUNE_COLOURS` tints a filled pip, and `socketPips` and `pipOffsets` say which pips an item shows and where, drawn under its icon by `socketPips.ts`'s `createPips`, which `InventoryScene` uses in its slots, backpack and ground and `TradingPostScene` and `CraftingScene` in their lists) (`itemIcons.ts`, which also has the pale tint a tier-2 material lays over its family's icon, `MATERIAL_TINTS` and `iconTint`, which the three scenes apply to every item icon they draw). `WorldScene` shows one atlas sprite per `LocationKind`, keyed by `LOCATION_SPRITES`, `map_watchtower` for a tower and `map_blight` on each corrupted hex, each playing its `idle`, and shows each party as its first member's battle figure, dressed in what it wears and holds, with its banner behind it (`BANNER_SPRITES` in `partyList.ts`, by banner number: `map_banner`, `map_banner_azure`, `map_banner_green`, each with its `idle`, which the party list also draws), parties sharing a hex spread across it (each banner on its outer side) and the selected one on top, a gold ring under its feet. `EncampmentScene` draws each facility from the atlas as `camp_<facility>/level_<n>` (by `FACILITY_SPRITES`; the guild hall has five, levels 4 and 5 the Barracks), or `camp_plot` while it is not built, on the 48px `building` canvas, with `camp_tent` and `camp_fire` for the look of the place. Every scene with a `PartyColumn`, and `Party` and `Creation`, loads one portrait per `PortraitId` from `assets/sprites/portrait_<id>.png` (`portraitKey`), so a new preset needs an id in `PORTRAIT_IDS` and a matching sprite in `art/portraits/`.

## Debug console and seeds

In dev builds `~` (or `` ` ``) opens a debug console over the top of the game (`debugConsole.ts`, plain HTML as the manual is, taking every key while open; `help` lists the commands, which live in `debugCommands.ts`). It works on the `DebugSource` of the scene in front (`debugSource()`, which `World` and `Battle` have): anything reads, but a command that changes the game (healing, setting hit points (`hp <amount> [member|all]`, to stage a death), experience, stock, items, facilities' levels (`facility guild_hall` up to 5), revealing the map, moving the party, passing days (`days`, through `passDay`, so each party carries out its order, and refused while one faces monsters), starting a battle of any encounter at any threat (`fight`, through the source's `fight`, which only `World` offers)) runs only on the world map, which redraws and saves after it, as a battle's engine would never hear of the change. A new command is an entry in `COMMANDS` there. A command that acts on a party (`heal`, `hp`, `xp`, `give`, `goto`, `fight`, `party`) takes an optional last argument `@<party>`, a party id (`party_1`) or the start of its name (`@first`), else it acts on the source's `party` (the scene's selected party) or the first; `party` lists the company (id, name, hex, order, confrontation, then the members). `give <item|rune> [count]` puts items in the party's baggage or, given a rune key (`storm1`), runes in the pouch; `items` lists the item ids, the rune keys and the `#` grammar, and `runes` lists the pouch. A battle `fight` starts has no location behind it: `Battle` is started with `encounter` and `threat` in its data instead (`chooseBattle`, used only without a `location`), and a win gives experience but clears no location, takes no loot and rolls no runes, leaves the blight alone and is never saved mid-battle.

The game takes a seed from the page's address, as in `http://localhost:5173/?seed=42` (`seed.ts`; `main.ts` puts it in the game's registry). Every battle of that session is then fought with it, the enemy's choices included, so the same actions play out the same way: use it to replay a battle from the seed its `battle_start` logged. Without one each battle draws a seed of its own, which its `battle_start` logs all the same.
