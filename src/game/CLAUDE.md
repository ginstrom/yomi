# The game outside battle

Loaded when working in `src/game/`: encounters and bandits, the company and its day loop, the world map, the blight, the encampment and its facilities, and saving. The simulator and the balance figures are in `scripts/CLAUDE.md`.

## The modules

- `src/game/encounters.ts`: battle setups, registered by id in `ENCOUNTERS`. A monster location on the world map names the `EncounterId` it leads to and the threat to fight it at, and the simulator fights `goblinRaid` unless told otherwise, so balance runs fight a battle the game has. A setup is built for the party and the threat it is given: the threat decides how many of the encounter's foes take the field and their level (see the rules of scaling below). The party is (a `Fighter` per member: stats and hit points left), lined up on the `PARTY_START` hexes and member i's unit is named `heroId(i)`, which is how `BattleScene` finds the `Character` behind a unit. Each setup also maps unit ids to a `UnitKind`, which is how scenes pick a sprite and how `xpReward` prices the foes (`FOE_XP` times their level); the engine knows only stats. A foe kind (`FoeKind`) is a monster (`MonsterKind`), a stat block scaled from its native level (`FOE_NATIVE_LEVEL`), or a bandit (`BanditKind`) (`src/game/bandits.ts`), built by hero rules: a role's draft made a character, dressed in its role's gear at the grade the threat calls for, levelled and spent by `autoAdvance`, its stats `deriveCombatStats`'s, so a bandit of level L fights as a hero of that draft and gear would; `foeStats(kind, level)` builds either. Each encounter has a band of threats (`ENCOUNTER_BANDS`) and a foe count for each threat in it (`ENCOUNTER_COUNTS`); a threat outside the band is fought as the nearest in it, and `encountersFor(threat)` lists the encounters whose band holds a threat (the blight's spawns draw from `SPAWN_KINDS` alone, so the newer nests do not spawn). `MAX_THREAT` (5) is today's map's highest threat and `threatAt`'s cap, no longer tied to the counts. The Bandit Camp (`bandit_camp`, threats 3 to 7) fields bandits, at two of the map's nests and in the Bandit Gangs the blight spawns from threat 3. The Spider Nest (`spider_nest`, threat 5 only, 4 foes: a Giant Spider, a Kobold Sneak, a Brood Spider and a Nest Spider, the fourth since skill trees step 24) and the Salamander Pool (`salamander_pool`, threat 5 only, 3 foes: a Salamander between two Kobolds) fight with abilities; the Giant Spider (native level 4) webs and bites, the Salamander (native 5) spits fire and is warded against it (`wards.fire` 99).
- `src/game/bandits.ts`: bandits, humanoid foes built by hero rules (`banditStats(role, level, gear)`, cached). Each of the six roles (`BanditRole`: cutthroat, archer, brute, mage, captain, crossbowman; `BANDIT_ROLE` maps the kinds to them) has a fixed, legal `CharacterDraft` and its gear: the Cutthroat an iron dagger, the Archer a yew bow, the Brute a battle axe and breastplate, the Hedge-Mage the Wand of Sparks (its draft takes Magic Arrow, Shield and Arcane Study; its plan, `bandit_mage`, adds Flame Arrow at level 3, Destruction at 4, Fireball at 7, Heal at 8 and Heal All at 10, so on today's map it never heals), the Captain a sword, oak shield and breastplate, the Crossbowman (the Archer's draft) a crossbow. Each is named by catalog family in two ids, tier 1 (iron, oak, yew) and tier 2 (steel, ironwood); the gear is at `banditGear(threat)`, `{ tier, grade }`: tier 1 Fine at threats 3 and 5 and plain at 4 (plain below 5 until skill trees step 24; threat 4 plain again since step 30, where perk points left a level-4 party a node short) and tier 2 Fine from 6 (the user's choice, amending stage 3's Q2: steel at 5 took the threat-5 camp cell from 469 to 256 of 500, and today's map ends at 5), the wand included, as it is a plain item. Bandits take their plan's techniques as they level (`autoAdvance` by `BANDIT_PLAN`): the Captain (`knight`, whose Heavy Hand comes after Pin) Rooted at 3, Taunt at 5 and Pin at 8; the Brute Power Attack at 3, Armour Breaker at 5 and Cleave at 8; the Cutthroat Backstab at 2, Dirty Trick at 4 and Sprint at 5; the Archer and Crossbowman Aimed Shot at 3, Volley at 6 and Far Shot at 8 (each held in `progression.test.ts`). A hero built the same way (`banditDraft`, the same gear, levels and `autoAdvance`) has the same stats, which a test holds it to.
- `src/game/party.ts`: the `Party`, the player's characters in battle order: the pre-rolled characters a place can be filled with (`PREROLLS`, each a draft and a short role shown under its portrait), the first three of which are the ready-made party (`READY_MADE`, `readyMadeParty`), a soldier, an archer and a mage; the party screen's limit, `STARTING_PARTY_LIMIT` (3), which no party may pass beyond `MAX_PARTY_SIZE` (6, what `PARTY_START` has hexes for); what a won battle brings the party (`rewardVictory`): experience for the living, while the fallen are dead (`buryFallen`, their gear into the baggage); and `autoHeal`, the spending of a party's mana on its wounds as a careful player would from the Abilities tab, which camping until healed does.
- `src/game/camp.ts`: camping on the world map, by a party (`campDay(game, party)`, a day of rest, a one-day order on the day loop (`act`, `company.ts`), and `campUntilHealed(game, party)`, a camp order run on it; `rest` is a day's healing with no time passing, which both orders use), the world map's way to restore mana and hit points; each day is a world turn (`passTurn`), and a notable event breaks camp. See the rules of the world map below.
- `src/game/blight.ts`: the blight (`docs/designs/blight.md`): every hex's level, the day's contest of influence over it (`advanceDay`), the blight's growth with time, spawning, and the cleansing a won battle brings (`cleanse`); the `WorldEvent`s a world turn returns. `src/game/territory.ts`: the resources held, and the encampment, captured and lost as their land's level changes, the warnings of a site under threat (`daysToFall`) and the yields to the encampment's stock (`updateTerritory`). The two and `worldMap.ts` import one another, which is safe while none calls another as its module loads. See the rules of the blight below.
- `src/game/buildings.ts`: what the encampment's stock builds, and the stock's arithmetic (`canAfford`, `spend`): the watchtower, built where a party stands (`canBuildTower(world, party)` gives the reason it cannot, `buildTower(game, party)` builds it as a one-day order on the day loop, `act` in `company.ts`, over `raiseTower`, which spends and raises it with no time passing); the shrine, raised a level at a time by a party at the encampment (`atEncampment(world, party)`, `raiseShrine`, no time passing), each level adding `SHRINE_STRENGTH` to the encampment; and the outpost, built by the party standing on a held resource (`buildOutpost`, a one-day order over `raiseOutpost`), adding `OUTPOST_STRENGTH` to the site and `OUTPOST_YIELD` to its yield. A tower is `WorldState.towers`, not a location: blight.ts counts it a clean source, vision.ts a watcher and territory.ts loses it as a held site. The shrine is `WorldState.shrine` and outposts `WorldState.outposts` (site ids), read by blight.ts's `siteStrength` and territory.ts's `siteYield`; an outpost falls with its site (`site_lost` with `outpost`).
- `src/game/guildHall.ts`: the guild hall at the encampment (`atGuildHall(world, party)`, a party standing there and facing no monsters): its level (`WorldState.guildHall`), which sets the party limit (`partyLimit`, derived, never stored: 3, 4, 5 at levels 1 to 3 and 5 after), how many parties the company may field (`maxParties`: 1, 1, 1, 2, 3 over its five levels, level 4 the Barracks) and how many heroes it may hold, parties and reserves together (`companyLimit`: 7, 8, 9, 14, 18, which `recruitRefusal` also checks), its upgrade for the stock, recruiting into the reserves (from a pre-roll or a created draft, at `recruitLevel`: the hall's level, with the levels' points unspent), and moving heroes between the party and the reserves. `homeParties` lists the parties at the encampment and `homeParty(game, preferred?)` picks the one the encampment acts for: the preferred (the scene's selected party) if it is at home, else the first at home. Each action returns why it was refused, or null; the company's own actions (`formParty`, `renameParty`, `disband`, `moveHero`, in `company.ts`) check everything first.
- `src/game/tradingPost.ts`: the trading post at the encampment (`WorldState.tradingPost`, built from the start at 1): its levels (1 to 3 sell tier 1 at grades up to the level, 4 adds tier 2 at every grade, `sells`); `wares(world, filter)` lists what it sells, narrowed by slot or family; buying plain items at a grade into the baggage, priced by family (`PRICES`) times the tier's and the grade's multipliers (`TIER_PRICE`, `GRADE_PRICE`), selling from the baggage and backpacks (never what is worn) for half, a modifier adding nothing, an enchanted item half its `VALUES` and an embedded rune half its price on top (`salePrice`; the post never sells a runed item); rune wares (`runeWares`: Ember, Rime, Storm, Stone, Wind and Life at rank 1 from level 2 and rank 2 from level 4, `RUNE_PRICES` 15, 45, 135, 405 by rank), `buyRune` into the pouch (`GameState.runes`) and `sellRune` from it for a third, and selling materials two for a gold piece; it never sells materials. `src/game/crafting.ts`: crafting, putting a modifier at a grade on an item where it lies (`craft`), replacing any it had: in the field (`craftTier` is `'field'` anywhere the party may camp, the encampment without a workshop included), the field modifiers at grade 1 and a day, a one-day order on the day loop (`act`, `company.ts`, over `craftRefusal` and `applyCraft`, which take no time); at the workshop (`WorldState.workshop`), the modifiers its level unlocks up to the grade of its level, no time passing. `src/game/runecraft.ts`: runecraft, runes moved between the pouch (`GameState.runes`) and an item's sockets where it lies: `embed` fills the next empty socket, at the workshop of any level for any rank, or a rank-1 rune in the field (where the party may camp) for a day, a one-day order on the day loop (`act`, over `embedRefusal` and `applyEmbed`); `fuse` makes three of a rune one of the next rank, to rank 2 from workshop level 1 (ranks 3 and 4 ask levels 4 and 5, which stage 10 adds, so they are refused for now, the refusal naming the level, checked before the count); `extract` takes a rune back intact from workshop level 2, the rest closing up; embedding costs 5 gold a rank, fusing 10 a rank of the new rune and extracting 10 a rank; no time passes at the workshop; a change to a worn item keeps its wearer's share of hit points (`replaceAt` ends with `clampHp`). `src/game/infirmary.ts`: the infirmary (`WorldState.infirmary`) and `restHealing`, what a day's camping heals a member where the party stands, which `campDay` uses. `src/game/itemPlace.ts`: `ItemPlace`, where an item the trading post or crafting acts on lies (baggage, worn, backpack) in the party it names (`party`, an id) with the id expected there, so a place that no longer holds it, or whose party is unknown, is refused as stale (`placeKey` leaves the party out); functions that list, buy or recruit take the acting party after `game`, and `allPlaces(game, party, worn?)` lists one party's places; a worn item replaced or removed through it ends with `clampHp` on the wearer, as a Life rune may move the maximum. Each action returns why it was refused, or null, and checks everything before changing anything.
- `src/game/loot.ts`: rune loot after a win (`takeRunes` into the pouch), drawn from a stream of its own seeded from the battle's seed (`lootRng`), so the battle's dice are untouched: by threat, a nest's leader favouring its element (`FAVOURED_RUNES`); the rows past threat 7 and the Source's own wait for stage 9 (the Source rolls threat 5's). `BattleScene.endBattle` calls `takeRunes` for a win at a monster location, and the simulator after each win; a debug `fight` rolls none.
- `src/game/encampment.ts`: what the encampment screen shows: every facility (`FACILITIES`: the guild hall, the shrine, the trading post, the workshop, the infirmary), built or not, its level, what it does and what its next level costs, and `improveFacility`, which calls the facility's own module for the party at home (`homeParty`: the selected party if it is there, else the first at home; with none at home it refuses). A new facility needs an entry there, a sprite and a plot (`encampmentView.ts`), and a scene in `EncampmentScene`'s `OPENS` if it has a screen of its own.
- `src/game/expedition.ts`: the company's parties: the `Expedition` (id, name, members, baggage, the hex it stands on (`position`), the monster location it walked into and has not answered (`confronting`) and a standing `Order`, which `company.ts`'s day loop runs (idle, travel, home, camp, garrison, meet, or a one-day action, `DayAction`); ids are `partyId(n)`, `party_1` to `party_3` (`MAX_PARTIES`), and default names `PARTY_NAMES` by number; the first's stored name is "First Company" while the map shows "Your party"), and the parties as the world's rules see them: `PartyPresence`, a party's hex and its members' levels (`presenceOf`, and `presences(game.parties)`, the company's in list order).
- `src/game/company.ts`: the company's one clock: each party's standing order (`Order`: idle, travel, home (a travel order to the encampment), camp, garrison (on a held resource, never ending by itself), meet (a hex a day toward another party's current hex, re-planned daily, ending with `met` on a shared hex and confronting any monsters there, confronting monsters it stepped onto as the other walked off, or idle with `order_done` once that party is gone or no way to it is left), or a one-day action; `setOrder`, which refuses a travel order to a monster location another party already stands on (a meet joins it there instead); a travel or home order stops a hex short, idle with `order_done`, of a monster location another party stands on, so of two parties reaching one nest on a day the earlier in the list confronts it) run a day at a time (idle and garrisoned parties away from monsters heal each living member `NATURAL_HEALING_PER_DAY` (1), capped at maximum HP, without mana recovery or infirmary bonuses; `passDay`: every party's day by its order, in list order, then `passTurn` once, then each order checked against the day), and `advance`, days until an `Interrupt` (the first notable world event, else a party's own event: arrived, confronting, order done, met), the game ending or `UNTIL_LIMIT` (30) days; `travel` (worldMap.ts, over `startTravel`, which gives the travel order and handles a refused one or a nest on its first hex, passing no day) and `campUntilHealed` (camp.ts) are conveniences over it, giving an order and advancing; `orderAction` gives a party a one-day action (scout, wait, a day's camp, a tower, an outpost, a field craft or embed) as a `day` order for the next day to run, refusing, changing nothing, what the action's own check or `advanceRefusal` refuses (`blockingParty` is the party whose monsters it refuses for, and `partyNoun` how a refusal names a party: "The party" alone, else its name), and `act` is `orderAction` then the day passed, and `linger`, `campDay`, `buildTower`, `buildOutpost` and the field `craft` and `embed` are conveniences over it. It and those modules (worldMap.ts, camp.ts, buildings.ts, crafting.ts, runecraft.ts) import one another, which is safe while none calls another as its module loads; it calls only their time-free parts and refusals. It also holds the guild hall's company actions, each needing the parties it touches at home: `formParty` (a party from a hero of a home party or the reserves, under the lowest free id and a name of 1 to 16 letters unique among the parties, within `maxParties`), `renameParty` (anywhere), `disband` (members to the reserves, baggage to the first other party at home, never the last party) and `moveHero` (between home parties and the reserves). And two parties' business in the field: `exchange` (members, swaps and baggage items between two parties on one hex, all its moves checked first, refused while either faces monsters the other does not; no reserve is reachable through it) and `wipeParty` (a party beaten in battle: its heroes dead, what they wore and carried and its baggage put on the location's loot, or lost with no location, the party gone; the last party wiped with reserves left is replaced at once by one formed from them at the encampment; `lost` when `heroesLeft`, every party's members and the reserves, is 0). It imports guildHall.ts, which never imports it.
- `src/game/vision.ts`: fog of war, what the player knows of the world map: the hexes in sight of every presence and watcher (`visibleHexes`), and `updateSight`, which every change to the land ends with, remembering what is in sight and telling of monsters newly sighted. Knowledge only: the rules run on the true state and the simulator ignores it.
- `src/game/ground.ts`: `Ground`, the loose items lying on a battlefield, one pile per hex, and `pileChanges`, which tells what a visit to the inventory dropped and picked up. `BattleScene` holds one `Ground` per battle; the engine does not track it; `fromPiles` puts a saved battle's back.
- `src/game/worldMap.ts`: the world map's state and rules, Phaser-free like the battle's: `WorldState` (the board, the turn count, the locations, each monster location with its fixed `loot`, the blight's levels and counters, what the player has seen of them (`seen`, `sighted`), the held sites, the stock, the world RNG's state and the game's `status`; where a party stands and what it confronts are its expedition's) and the functions that change it (`passTurn`, through which every turn passes; `travel(game, party, to)` (`walkTo` gives its days' reports too, and `followOrder` walks a home or meet order the same way), `linger(game, party, how)`, `clearLocation(game, id)`, which frees every party confronting it). The world's rules (`passTurn`, `isNotable`, and blight.ts's, territory.ts's and vision.ts's) take the parties as a list of presences (`PartyPresence`, expedition.ts), whose levels are their influence, passed as `presences(game.parties)`; the rules of where a party stands take its expedition (read-only checks `(world, party)`, as `monstersHere`, `routeTo`, `canCamp`, `restHealing`, `craftTier`; anything that passes a day `(game, party)`). `WorldScene` asks `routeTo` what a click would do and must not decide it. See the rules below.
- `src/game/gameState.ts`: state that outlives a scene, which is the company's `parties` (each an `Expedition`, `expedition.ts`: its `members` (the `Party`), its `baggage`, a shared pile of items with no limit that `takeLoot` moves a beaten location's loot into, its `position`, its `confronting` and its `order`; one to `MAX_PARTIES` (3) of them, never none while a hero lives; a reader takes the party it acts for explicitly, from `partyById` or `selectedParty(game, id)`, the scenes' choice from their scene data, falling back to the first, and a company-wide reader works over `game.parties`; `takeLoot(party, location)` puts loot in that party's baggage), the `WorldState` (of a random world seed unless `newGame` is given one), the rune pouch `runes` (`Pouch`, saved, validated on load), the `reserves` waiting at the guild hall, the `story` (below) and the save `slot` it lives in. It is created by the party screen, or read from a slot, and passed through scene data, never held in a module global. A game with no slot (`newGame()`'s default) is never saved: the simulator's, a test's.
- `src/game/save.ts` and `src/game/saveSlots.ts`: the save format (a `SaveFile` to a string and back, storage-free) and the three slots in `localStorage` that hold the strings, reached through `src/storage.ts`, the guarded `localStorage` the audio settings share. See the rules of saving below.

## Rules of the encampment's gear

See `docs/designs/expeditions-and-encampment.md`. A plain item's grade comes from the trading post and its modifier from crafting; a grade adds damage (and at Masterwork, to hit) to a weapon, armour class to a shield or a small-slot item and resistance to body armour, and a modifier trades one stat for another (Bearded a point of piercing a grade, Reinforced a point of resistance), Light alone moving a weapon's AP (the longsword and axe to 4 and 5); nothing moves the bow off 4 AP. Gold is the contested resource: the guild hall, recruits, the shrine, gear and crafts all want it, and the far materials (stone, copper, silver, crystal, peat, salt) buy the higher levels of the trading post, workshop and infirmary and the higher grades of craft. A damage bonus a modifier lowers may go below 0, so a hit's damage is kept at 0 or more (`rollHitDamage`).

## Rules of the world map

The encampment is the only settlement (`kind: 'encampment'`, `ENCAMPMENT_ID`), named by the player when the game begins (its location's `name`, read through `encampmentName`, never assumed to be Oakvale), and every other quiet location is a resource; the map (`WORLD_SIZE`) is several windows across, and `WorldScene` scrolls it to follow the party, or where a drag on it pans it (a press that moves no more than `DRAG_THRESHOLD_PX` is a click to travel), until the next journey, Home or a click on the party's entry in the list down its right brings it back to the selected party (`centreOnSelected`); time passes in turns, one per hex travelled, and every turn is the blight's day (`passTurn`); a journey and camping until healed pass their days through the day loop (`passDay`, `company.ts`: each party's order, then `passTurn` once), as orders run until an interrupt, and an order outlasts the interrupt (a cut-short walk or camp is still the party's order, and the next day carries it on), a travel order keeping the route planned when it was given, and every one-day action (scouting, waiting, a day's camp, building a tower or an outpost, a field craft or embed) is a one-day order through the same loop (`act`), after which the party is idle; each party has its own order, and a party may be sent home (`home`, from anywhere away from the encampment), set to garrison a held resource it stands on (`garrison`, which holds the site only as any party's presence does: its hex is neutral and its levels push the blight back) or sent to meet another party (`meet`, by a click on that party's hex), and two parties sharing a hex may trade heroes and baggage (`exchange`, the `Exchange` scene); a travel order to a nest another party stands at is refused, and one that finds another party there first stops a hex short; fog of war hides the rest (`vision.ts`, `docs/designs/guild-hall-and-watchtowers.md`): each party, the encampment, each held site and each watchtower see every hex within a radius (`PARTY_VISION` 5, `CAMP_VISION` 6, a resource 2, a tower 8; nothing blocks sight), a hex once seen is remembered at the level it last had in sight (`WorldState.seen`, -1 never seen) and a monster location once seen stays known (`sighted`); the Source is unknown at the start too, a mystery found only by exploring, and its sighting (`source` on the `sighted` event) is notable however far off; a journey is walked a hex and a turn at a time and stops where a notable event happens (`isNotable`: monsters sighted within `NOTICE_RADIUS` of some party's presence, the Source wherever it is sighted, a held site or tower newly under threat or lost, the encampment lost; a spawn is told only when sighted) or where monsters stand on the road; routes keep clear of every sighted monster location except one they end on, and go through the unexplored as through open land; walking into monsters sets the party's `confronting`, and it cannot travel on until it scouts, waits (a turn each, and neither does more yet) or attacks; on a held resource the party may build an outpost (`buildOutpost`, a day, `OUTPOST_COST`); anywhere but among monsters the party may build a watchtower on clean land off every location, `TOWER_SPACING` (4) hexes from any other, for `TOWER_COST` from the stock wherever the party is, in a day (`buildTower`), and may camp (`camp.ts`), a turn a day, each day healing every member `NATURAL_HEALING_PER_DAY` (1), or more with the infirmary (`restHealing`: a third, then all of it at the encampment, then 3 a day elsewhere), and filling every caster's mana, and camping until healed casts the party's heals with the mana it has (`autoHeal`), then camps a day and casts again while anyone is wounded, unless a notable event breaks camp; a won battle takes its location off the map, cleanses the land around it and puts its loot in the baggage (`BattleScene.endBattle` calls `takeLoot` and `clearLocation`, and the victory screen names what was found). Nothing is done in time but the turn: the game ends only when the encampment falls (`status` 'lost', permadeath as a defeat that leaves no hero is) or the Source is beaten ('won'), after which the player ends the game, keeping the save, or goes on in 'endless' mode.

## Rules of the blight

See `docs/designs/blight.md`. Every hex has a level, 0 (clean) to 3 (corrupted), kept densely in `WorldState.blight` by `boardIndex`. Each day each hex's `shift` counter moves by its net influence, blight less clean, and its level by one when the counter reaches `SHIFT_COST` either way. Each source pushes with its strength less a point a hex of distance: a monster location with `BLIGHT_PER_THREAT` × its threat, the Source with `SOURCE_STRENGTH`, each corrupted neighbour with `CREEP`, all times the blight's growth with time (`blightGrowth`: a tenth more every 30 days, to half again at day 151, worked out from the turn); against them the encampment (`CAMP_STRENGTH`, more with its shrine), each held resource (more with an outpost), each watchtower (`TOWER_STRENGTH`), and each party (each presence, in list order), with `PARTY_BASE` and half its levels together (`partyStrength`; nothing else of a party counts). A monster location's hex stays blighted or worse until it is beaten; each party's hex is neutral, so the blight never rises there nor creeps from it, but the parties' influence lowers it. The fixed sources' influence is worked out again only when a location or a held site changes (`fields`, a memo outside the state). Corrupted land builds spawn pressure, and each `SPAWN_COST` of it puts a monster location on corrupted land clear of every party, the nests and the held sites, drawn with the world RNG (whose state is saved), at the threat of where it lies (`threatAt`, never the blight or the date, so the home band's nests stay ones a weak party can beat), its kind drawn among those whose encounter band holds that threat (a Goblin Brood, a Kobold Nest, and from threat 3 a Bandit Gang), up to `MAX_MONSTER_LOCATIONS`. A won battle lowers the land within 1 + its threat by two levels. A resource is held while its land is clean enough: captured at level 0, lost at `LOSS_LEVEL` (2), under threat (with the days until it falls) while the blight is gaining on it; every `YIELD_DAYS` each held site adds its yield to the encampment's `stock`, half if tainted, which the buildings (buildings.ts) and the guild hall spend. A watchtower is lost, and warned of, as a held site is. Spawns keep `SPAWN_CLEARANCE` from towers too. The Source (`SOURCE_ID`, far in the east) fights its own encounter at threat 5 (`source_guardians`): the Blight Horror, a boss whose claws pierce 2 points of resistance and which casts Death Cloud and Bane, with a Goblin Brute, a shaman and a Kobold Sneak. The constants were tuned with the campaign simulator, not by design.

## Rules of saving

There are three slots (`yomi.save.1` to `3` in `localStorage`) and a playthrough lives in one from Begin to its end, written by the game itself (`saveGame`); there is no save button and only one save of a playthrough. `WorldScene` saves as it opens and after everything that changes the game (a journey, as it is begun, and Next day and Until something happens likewise, before their walk plays; scouting, waiting or camping; the closing of the character panel). `BattleScene` saves each time the player's turn begins, the first included, with the battle: the party fighting (`SavedBattle.party`, an expedition id that `parseSave` checks against the game's parties and a load passes to `Battle`, so a reload fights that party and not the first), the engine's snapshot, where the enemy's RNG stands and the ground's piles; a reload therefore rewinds to the start of the turn, with the same dice. A victory is saved as it happens, before the victory screen is left; a defeat that leaves no hero in any party or the reserves deletes the slot (permadeath) and clears the game's `slot` so that nothing writes it back, and so does the encampment's fall, on the world map; a defeat with heroes left elsewhere is saved as it happens, with no battle, the wiped party gone. A loaded battle builds its encounter again from the location, for the units' kinds and the experience it is worth, and takes everything else from the save. In the saved string every `Item` is its id, put back by `itemById` as the constant or the graded or modified item it builds and remembers, so identity comparisons hold after a load and an item's tuning reaches old saves; a new base or enchanted item must be in `BASE_ITEMS` or `MAGIC_ITEMS` or a game holding it cannot be loaded. Raise `SAVE_VERSION` whenever the shape of `GameState`, `Character`, `WorldState` or `BattleSnapshot` changes. ## Rules of scaling

Monsters are the more dangerous the further from the party's home they lie. A monster location's `threat` is 1 within `THREAT_BAND` (11) hexes of the encampment and one more for each band beyond, up to `MAX_THREAT` (5) (`threatAt`), and `WorldScene` tells the player the threat of the monsters it stands before. Each encounter has a band of threats (`ENCOUNTER_BANDS`, `encountersFor`; the monsters' five are 1 to 5, the Bandit Camp 3 to 7, the Spider Nest 5 only) and a foe count for each threat in it (the monsters' five 2, 3, 3, 3, 4; the Bandit Camp 2, 2, 2 at threats 3 to 5 and 4, 4 at 6 and 7, a first guess for stage 9), and a threat outside the band is fought as the nearest in it. A battle at threat T fields that many foes, all of level T, the first of the encounter's slots that take the field at T (`FoeSlot.fromThreat`). A monster kind has a native level (`FOE_NATIVE_LEVEL`, 1 but for the Giant Spider's 4 and the Salamander's 5; `foeStats(kind, level)` builds a foe), its stat block written for that level; a level above it adds 115% of its level 1 hit points (`FOE_HP_PER_LEVEL`; 130% from skill trees step 24 to step 30), 5 to chance to hit and to armour class, 1 damage every second level, and to a caster 3 mana and 5 to its spells' chance (`foeAtLevel`); resistance and regeneration are the kind's at every level. `foeYardstick(level)` gives the ranges a foe of a level should sit in, which a test holds each kind to at its native level. Goblin Raid's third foe is the Goblin Shaman (so it fights from threat 2) and its fourth the Kobold Sneak. The armoured foes are the Hobgoblin Chief (resistance 4, the Hobgoblin Warband, threat 2 on the map) and, in the Troll Lair at threat 5 only, the Armoured Troll (resistance 5, regeneration 2) beside a Troll (resistance 1, regeneration 3, `troll_claws` as one 8 AP routine standing still or one 4 AP claw after a move); the simulator's heroes never change weapons, so the Troll Lair is a report-only cell (won 0 or nearly 0 of 500 at threat 5, level 5, whatever weapon or rune is put in every hand: a rank-3 Ember won 2). The Salamander Pool and the Spider Nest (both threat 5, not among the spawns) fight with abilities; the Salamander's fire ward 99 makes Flame Arrow and Fireball deal it 0, though statuses land, and the policy's area rule counts only foes a spell can hurt. Bandits (`bandits.ts`) hold the Bandit Camp (threat 3) and Hideout (threat 4) and spawn as Bandit Gangs from threat 3. The camp's slots are the Captain, the Archer (from threat 5), the Hedge-Mage (from 4), the Crossbowman (from 6), the Brute and the Cutthroat, so each threat of today's map has its own second bandit (Brute at 3, Hedge-Mage at 4, Archer at 5); they wear Fine iron and yew (tier 1) at threats 3 and 5, plain iron and yew at 4, and Fine steel and ironwood (tier 2) from 6 (steel at 5 took the cell from 469 to 256), and at 6 and 7 four take the field, the Brute and Cutthroat only once a later stage raises a count to 5 or 6.
