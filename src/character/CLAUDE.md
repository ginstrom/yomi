# Characters, gear and progression

Loaded when working in `src/character/` (and pointed to from `src/inventory/CLAUDE.md`). Characters follow Fallout 1 and 2; the skill trees' design is `docs/designs/skill-trees.md`.

## The modules

- `src/character/` and `src/inventory/`: a persistent `Character` and its equipment. A character stores only what it was born with (`attributes`, `tagged` skills), what it has learned (`level`, `trained` skill points, `perks`), what it has left (`hp`, and `mana`, read through `manaOf`) and what it carries; every number it fights with is derived from those, never stored: `maxHp`, `maxMana`, and `deriveCombatStats`, which turns a character into the `CombatantStats` the engine fights with. That holds for attributes too: `character.attributes` is what it was born with, and every reader goes through `attributeLevel` (`perks.ts`), which adds what its perks have raised, never past 10. `attributes.ts`, `skills.ts` and `perks.ts` are the three tables, with the rules for raising a skill and qualifying for a perk (a node of the skill trees); `trees.ts` holds the trees, their paths and tiers, the depth, the calling and the capstone (see the rules of perks below); `skills.ts` and `perks.ts` import each other, and so do `perks.ts` and `trees.ts`, which is safe only while neither calls the other as its module loads. `creation.ts` is character creation: a `CharacterDraft` is what the player chose (attributes, tag, skill points placed, perks in the order taken, name, portrait) and `draftCharacter` the level 1 `Character` it makes; the screens call its functions and decide nothing. `catalog.ts` is the catalog of plain gear: the families (`FAMILIES`, 28: weapons, shields, body armour and the head, gloves and boots slots, each with its slot, material kind, icon and tier-1 stats, among them what it asks of strength (`strength`) and takes in action points (`apPenalty`), a reach weapon's `reach` and the crossbow's `rangedPierce`, which `deriveCombatStats` derives (see the rules of characters)), the materials (`MATERIALS`, a tier 1 and a tier 2 of each kind: iron and steel, yew and ironwood, leather and wolfhide, linen and silk), what tier 2 adds by gear class (`TIER_BONUS`, `gearClass`: weapon, shield, armour, small) and the legacy ids (`LEGACY_ALIASES`). `inventory.ts` has the items: the plain items the trading post and crafting work with (`BASE_ITEMS`, generated as every family in each material of its kind, tier 1 first: 56, the Wand of Sparks among them as the wand's tier 1), the six plain weapons a new hero carries (`WEAPONS`) and the enchanted ones won as loot (`MAGIC_ITEMS`). A plain item's id is `<material>_<family>` (`steel_longsword`), or for a tier 1 that has its own the family's `tier1` id (`iron_sword`, `oak_shield`, `spark_wand`, `ash_staff`, `leather_armor`); the tier-1 greatsword and breastplate are `iron_greatsword` and `iron_breastplate`, `steel_greatsword` being tier 2's and `steel_armor` an alias of `iron_breastplate`, which builds the same object, carrying the canonical id. A plain item comes in three grades (`grades.ts`) and may carry one modifier (`modifiers.ts`, `MODIFIERS`: the families it fits, the workshop level that unlocks it, its stat change at each grade); such an item is named by its id, `<plain>[@<grade>][+<modifier><grade>][#<rune>(.<rune>)*]` (`iron_sword@2+light1`, `steel_longsword@3#ember2.storm1`; grade 1 is never written, so each item has one id; an enchanted item takes the `#` part only, `emberblade#dawn1`), and `itemById` builds it from that id and remembers it, so one id is always one object, refusing an id with more runes than the item's sockets or a rune that does not fit (`runeFits`: Mind in a weapon only if it strikes with Magic, Acid only if it is a melee weapon or the crossbow, whose piercing it adds to; `runeFitsOnly` says where the rune fits, for the refusal). Every plain item, graded, modified or runed, carries its `family`, `material` and `tier`, and a runed plain item gets `base` and any runed item its `runes` (in socket order, filled sockets first; `unruned`, `withRunes`, and `craftedItem`, which keeps them). A grade adds by gear class (damage to a weapon, armour class to a shield or a small-slot item, resistance to body armour) and never changes attack AP, range, piercing, strength or a penalty; no tier, grade or modifier touches maximum hit points (a Life rune does, see `clampHp`); enchanted items take no grade or modifier. `runes.ts` has the runes: ten kinds (`RUNES`) at four ranks, each doing one thing in a weapon (the main hand), another in armour (every other slot) and another in a shield (`slotClass`), folded into a runed item's stats (`addStats`); the sockets (`socketsOf`: 0 at tier 1 and 1 at tier 2, one more at Masterwork, at most 3, a small-slot item at most 1, and the Emberblade, the Wand of Storms and the Hawkeye Bow 1 of their own); the pouch's helpers, and the one phrasing of on-hit effects (`onHitParts`, long, or short for the character panel's narrow column), which every screen that tells them uses. A rune adds the `ItemStats` fields `wards` (by element), `onHit` (a weapon's flat on-hit damage, status or healing, `OnHit` in `combat.ts`) and `maxHp`, besides the old ones, which `deriveCombatStats` and `maxHp` derive (wards summed over everything worn, `onHit` from the main hand only, the worn items' `maxHp` added) and the engine resolves. `portraits.ts` lists the preset faces (`PORTRAIT_IDS`); a character's `portrait` is only looks. See the rules of characters below.
- `src/character/progression.ts`: levels and experience: what each level costs (`xpToNextLevel`), what `gainXp` does to a character, the perk points a level brings and what a node costs to take (`pointsAt`, `canAffordPerk`, `affordableAt`), and the spending of what a level brings (`takePerk`, and `autoAdvance` for the simulator and the bandits, which follows a plan, `AUTO_PLANS`, saving for its next node). See the rules below.
- `src/character/worldCasting.ts`: casting out of battle, which is only healing, on the world map: which heals a character can cast now (`worldSpells`) and the cast itself (`castOnWorldMap`), paid from the mana it carries and restoring the average of the spell's dice and the caster's heal bonus (Mending, `worldHealing`), so the world map rolls nothing.

## Rules of characters

A character has six attributes from 1 to 10 (`ATTRIBUTE_IDS`), adding up to `ATTRIBUTE_POINTS` (32) at birth, which is one short of three at 10: one at 10 leaves two at 7 and the rest low, two at 10 leave the others near 3; perks raise them from there, never past 10, and the sum is the attribute everywhere (`attributeLevel`). They give it hit points (10 + strength + endurance, and 2 + half its endurance a level), action points (5 + half its agility), armour class (agility, plus armour, shield and enchanted gear; its resistance is only what its armour stops), its critical chance (luck) and a little melee damage (strength), and they set where each skill starts. There are three skills, each a percentage (`SKILLS`): Melee Weapons, Archery and Magic. They start low, from attributes alone (strength + agility, perception + agility, twice intelligence), so that the rest is the character's choices: one skill is tagged, which adds `TAG_BONUS` (20) and doubles what a skill point buys, and a raise costs 2 points from 80% and 3 from 90% (`SKILL_COST_TIERS`); skill points raise no skill past `MAX_SKILL` (100%), `skillRaiseRefusal` saying why, though a node's 10% may carry it past (the cap moved no campaign of the default run, whose heroes and bandits stay below it; `--ready-level 10` runs move slightly). `skillLevel` is the skill everywhere, perks' share included. An untagged, untrained skill sits near 10%, which the engine's floor makes a 5% chance against anything armoured. A weapon skill is the chance to hit with that kind of weapon before the foe's armour (`weaponSkill`: Melee Weapons, bare hands included, which roll a fixed 1d4; Archery for a weapon that reaches further than a hex and has no reach; or the skill the weapon names, which for a wand and a staff is Magic). Gear has weight (`deriveCombatStats`): each worn item that asks more strength (`ItemStats.strength`, read against `attributeLevel`, so Gain Strength helps) than the wearer has costs `SHORTFALL_TO_HIT` (5) to hit a point short, item by item and added up (`strengthShortfall`; an off-hand item under a two-handed weapon counts for nothing), against weapon attacks only; an item's `apPenalty` comes off action points every turn (plate and the tower shield 1 each); heavy gear's negative `spellSkill` lowers spells (plate −15, the breastplate, helm and gauntlets −5), which is how weight tells against a caster. Runes and nodes (Spell Ward, Blessed Armour) give a hero wards (`CombatantStats.wards`, summed over everything worn and the nodes held), which take their points off each hit and tick of their element as a monster's do. The iron breastplate every hero starts in asks strength 3 (the user's decision, after 4 cost the strength-3 ready-made archer and mage 5 to hit and the guard 56 campaigns), so those two lose nothing to it, and a wearer of strength 2 loses 5. A wand is in every other respect a ranged weapon: no mana, no zone of control, nothing from strength, and the archer's perks apply. Magic is also the chance of a spell striking, and what spell nodes ask for. There are no classes: every path of the skill trees is open to every hero.

## Rules of creation

See `creation.ts`. All six attributes start at 1 and the player places 26 points (`CREATION_ATTRIBUTE_POINTS`, so they add up to `ATTRIBUTE_POINTS` as ever), all of them, none above 10; tags one skill and places 10 skill points (`CREATION_SKILL_POINTS`); spends 3 perk points (`CREATION_PERK_POINTS`) on nodes of the skill trees' first tier (the only one open at level 1, each costing 1; `perkPointsLeft`); and gives a name (at most 12 letters, unique in the party, as the battle log tells units apart by name) and one of the preset portraits. Creation has no rules of its own about what a point or a perk does: `draftCharacter` spends them with `raiseSkill` and `takePerk`, so nothing a perk asks is waived, and a perk must be open to a level 1 character. Skill points and perk points left unspent stay on the character, to spend on the world map as those of a level are. A change that leaves a chosen perk unqualified drops it and returns its point (`pruneDraft`), and the screen says which. Starting gear is not chosen: a character holds the weapon of its tagged skill (`STARTING_KIT`), wears an iron breastplate (whose spell skill −5 a caster pays) and carries every other plain weapon. A specialist (its skill tagged, all 10 points in it, its skill node's 10%) starts near 65% when its skill's attributes come to about 15 (strength 9 and agility 6, say), which is the trade-off: a 60% start leaves less for the rest. The prerolls, the ready-made three among them, are drafts like any other, held to the same rules by a test. The party being built is a list of drafts, which is what lets a character be edited until the game begins; the drafts are not kept after that.

## Rules of perks and spells

See the skill trees, `docs/designs/skill-trees.md`. A perk (`PERKS`) is a node of the skill trees (`trees.ts`): four trees (Warrior, Scout, Mage, Cleric) of three paths each (`PATHS`, `PATH_IDS`), every node data: its `path` and `tier`, what it asks beyond its tier (attributes, skills, `anySkill`, any one of several, worded "Melee 40% or Magic 40%", and a node of its own path it `requires`), what it adds (`PerkEffects`, which `perkBonus` sums wherever that number is derived), `meleeOnHit` and the `spell` it teaches, if any (`perkTeaching`). Every node has one rank (`perks` holds 1), and costs its tier's perk points (`perkCost`, from `TIERS`' `cost`, no node priced on its own; `nodesCost` sums the nodes held; `canAffordPerk`), which `takePerk` spends. A tier asks a level and a depth, the nodes already held in its path, and costs (`TIERS`: tier 1 level 1, none and 1 point, tier 2 level 2, 1 and 2, tier 3 level 6, 3 and 3, the capstone level 12, 5 and 4); depth counts nodes, not points; a capstone also asks that its path be the hero's calling, the path or paths it holds most nodes in (`calling`, derived, never stored, ties all counting), and that it hold no capstone (`capstoneOf`): taking one closes every other for good. Asks gate taking a node, never keeping it. `missingRequirements` words what is lacking in one order (level, attributes, skills, any skill, the node required, "3 nodes in Heavy Knight", "Heavy Knight must be your deepest path", "Only one capstone"), and `describePerkRequirements` the same out of context. `PERKS` is ordered by tree, path and tier, which is the order the tree view lays a path's nodes out in; a new node of an existing shape is one entry there. All 95 nodes of the design are in, each with its whole effect: 7 or 8 a path (Spellsword 7), two of tier 1 and one capstone in each; the 17 techniques, each a node that teaches an `ability` and worded from `ABILITIES` with its cost; 14 nodes that switch on a `trait` (`TRAIT_IDS`, the engine reading it by name: Rooted, the auras Guardian, Aura of Courage (`courage`) and Aegis, Tumble, Counter, Ambush, Deathblow, Blademaster, Deadeye, Unbreakable, Shadowdancer, Sanctuary and Arcane Knight); the rest effects or spells; the effects new to the trees (resistance, wards, a shield's armour class, spell power, heal bonus, extended statuses, friendly range, AP discounts and the rest) are derived (`deriveCombatStats`: resistance, every ward and blight's, Shield Wall's armour class only with a shield held under a one-handed weapon, the nodes' `meleeOnHit` after the runes' with a melee weapon or bare hands, and the spell modifiers on `magic`), and the stage-3 numbers, `opportunity` to `manaRegen`, and the nodes' `traits`). The skill nodes add 10% (Weapon Training, Marksmanship, Arcane Study, keyed by skill id and read by `skillLevel`, so they count towards what another node asks). Only Gain Strength raises an attribute alone (+2, refused above 8 by `cappedAttribute`); Toughness, Far Shot, Skirmisher, Fortune and Lasting Magic each add 1 to theirs beside their own effect, the attribute stopping at 10. A raise changes at once everything the attribute feeds, hit points of past levels included (they are derived), but not skill points already handed out (they are banked). A character knows exactly the spells its nodes teach (`knownSpells`); every hero has mana (`maxMana`: twice its intelligence, half its intelligence more a level, `MANA_PER_TREE_NODE` (2) for each node it holds in the Mage and Cleric trees, and what its gear and nodes add), but only one that knows a spell, or a technique that costs mana, brings `magic` into battle (its `spells` then maybe empty); `deriveCombatStats` also gives a hero its `abilities` and `imbueDamage` (the `imbueDamage` effect) when it has any. An item's `spellSkill` helps spells strike but counts neither towards what a node asks nor for a wand. There are no spell picks and no Spellcasting: a spell is a node bought with perk points like any other, its Magic ask the gate (40% at tier 1, 55% at 2, 65 to 70% at 3, 80% for a capstone). `nodeKind` tells a spell, a technique used at will or for mana (`Perk.ability`, by its `manaCost`) and a passive apart. The trees are drawn by one view (`PerkTreeView`), with a tab for each tree (or keys 1 to 4), in the character panel and in creation alike.

## Rules of progression

Winning a battle earns the experience its foes are worth together (`xpReward`), a foe of level L being worth L times what its kind is worth at level 1, awarded by `BattleScene` when the battle ends (`rewardVictory`); the engine and the simulator know nothing of it. Every living party member earns the whole reward, so the yardstick below holds for each hero whatever the party's size. A member who fell in a won battle is dead (permadeath): out of the party for good, what they wore and carried put in the baggage, and named on the victory screen; the party fights on smaller until the guild hall fills the place. A battle is lost only when every member has fallen, and it wipes the party out (`wipeParty`): its heroes are dead, what they wore and carried and its baggage lie at the location as its loot, and the party is gone; the game ends only when no hero is left in any party or the reserves, and the last party wiped with reserves left is replaced at once by one formed from them at the encampment (up to the party limit, the lowest free id and its default name). Reserves earn nothing. Wounds are carried from battle to battle; a new level heals fully, and otherwise hit points come back by healing spells, in battle or on the world map, and by camping, a hit point a day; mana spent on the world map is refilled by a day of camping, but not by waiting or travel. A foe's worth is fixed by its kind and level, while each level costs more than the last, so early levels come fast and later ones slowly. The yardstick is the even foe, worth `EVEN_FOE_XP_PER_LEVEL` × the hero's level, with a weak foe at half that: level 2 costs a little under one even foe, and after that leaving level L costs L − 1 of them. Price a new monster against that yardstick. A level brings hit points, skill points (2 + a third of the hero's intelligence, so 2 to 5, kept few because a tagged skill doubles them) and a perk point (`PERK_POINTS_PER_LEVEL`, 1, at every level: `pointsAt(level)`, the three of creation and one a level, 6 by level 4, 10 by 8, 14 by 12, 22 by 20; for a hero never migrated, `perkPoints + nodesCost` is always `pointsAt(level)`); it heals the hero fully and fills its mana. A hero that stays in one path takes its first T3 at level 6 and its capstone at 12 with nothing to spare; one who mixes paths holds more nodes but reaches a capstone later. `autoAdvance` spends a simulated hero's, or a bandit's, by a plan (`AUTO_PLANS`, `planFor` by the tagged skill: Melee `knight`, Archery `ranger`, Magic `evoker_healer`; a bandit's role names its own, `BANDIT_PLAN`; a parity plan, a path's nodes, names its capstone first, so that it is saved for and taken as soon as it is afforded, the rest in `PERKS` order but for the cryomancer's, which takes Ice Lance before Web): again and again it picks a target, the first node of the plan it qualifies for or would if only its level were higher, else the first in `PERKS` order it qualifies for now that is no capstone, and stops, saving its points, while the target is short of the level or of points, never spending them on a cheaper node further down (a node short of depth, a skill, an attribute or the node it requires is skipped, as waiting will not open it). The ready-made heroes come to Rooted 3, Taunt 5, Pin 8, Shield Wall 9 and Unbreakable 13 (Aldric), Aimed Shot 3, Volley 6, Far Shot 8 and Deadeye 12 (Brenna), Flame Arrow 3, Arcane Blood 4, Heal All 6, Fireball 9, Pyromancy 11 and Meteor 15 (Delwyn; skill trees step 30 moved Arcane Blood and Heal All ahead of Fireball, for the level-4 Bandit Camp and the days spent camping). The skill points and perk points wait on the character until the player spends them, which is done on the world map and not in battle: stats changed mid-battle would have to reach the engine as an `equip` action does. A node is chosen from the trees and nowhere else.
